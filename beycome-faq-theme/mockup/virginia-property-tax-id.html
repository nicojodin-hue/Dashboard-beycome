Virginia Property Tax ID Number: How to Find and Use It

Updated April 21, 2026 · 7 min read

Every parcel of real property in Virginia is assigned a unique identification number by the local government — whether a county or one of Virginia's 38 independent cities. This number — called a parcel ID, parcel number, map reference number, or account number — is the key to your property's complete public record: assessed value, tax history, ownership transfers, and relief program status. Virginia's property tax system is unusually local: rates, exemptions, and even the name of the taxing office vary from jurisdiction to jurisdiction. This guide shows you how to find your Virginia property tax ID and use it to understand and manage your tax bill.

What Is a Virginia Property Tax ID Number?

A Virginia property tax ID is assigned by the local Commissioner of the Revenue or, in some localities, the Real Estate Assessor's office. Virginia splits assessment and collection between two constitutional officers: the Commissioner of the Revenue (who assesses values) and the Treasurer (who collects taxes). The parcel ID appears on every document tied to the property: tax bills, deeds, recorded plats, and closing disclosures.

Virginia is also unique in having 38 independent cities that are legally separate from any county — each administers its own property tax system. Knowing whether your property is in a county or an independent city matters, because they have different offices, websites, and tax rates.

The parcel number may also be called:

  • Parcel ID or parcel number
  • Map reference number or map/parcel
  • Tax account number or GPIN (Geographic Parcel Identification Number)
  • Real estate account number
  • Land book number

How to Find Your Virginia Property Tax ID

All four methods below are free.

1. Check Your Property Tax Bill

Virginia localities mail real estate tax bills one or two times per year (depending on the jurisdiction). Your parcel ID is printed on the bill, labeled "Parcel ID," "Map Number," "Account Number," or similar. This is the most authoritative source for the exact format your locality uses.

2. Search Your Locality's Commissioner of the Revenue or Assessor Website

Every Virginia county and independent city provides a free online property search. Enter your street address or owner name to retrieve your parcel ID, assessed value, tax history, and ownership information. Larger jurisdictions like Fairfax County, Virginia Beach, and Richmond have sophisticated GIS portals; smaller jurisdictions often use platforms like qPublic, Vision Government Solutions, or county-built systems.

3. Use the Virginia GIS Network

The Virginia Geographic Information Network (VGIN) maintains a statewide parcel viewer that aggregates parcel data from many localities. You can search by address or click on a map to retrieve a parcel ID and link to the local assessor's records.

4. Check Your Deed or Closing Documents

Your recorded deed and closing disclosure both reference the parcel ID. In Virginia, deeds are recorded with the Circuit Court Clerk's office (not a county recorder as in most states). Your title commitment and HUD-1 or Closing Disclosure also list the parcel ID.

Virginia Property Tax Rates by Locality

Virginia real property is assessed at 100% of fair market value — there is no fractional assessment ratio as in Alabama or Ohio. Tax rates are set by each locality independently, expressed as dollars per $100 of assessed value. The state of Virginia does not levy a real property tax.

How Virginia Property Taxes Are Calculated

The calculation is straightforward — Virginia assesses at full market value with no ratio adjustment.

Step 1: Determine Assessed Value

The local Commissioner of the Revenue or assessor estimates the property's fair market value as of January 1 each year. Virginia law requires localities to assess at 100% of fair market value. Reassessment schedules vary: cities generally reassess annually, while counties may reassess every 2, 4, or 6 years depending on population and local ordinance.

Step 2: Subtract Exemptions or Land Use Deductions

If you qualify for a local relief program — elderly/disabled tax relief, land use taxation, or disabled veteran exemption — the eligible amount is subtracted from the assessed value before the tax rate is applied.

Step 3: Apply the Tax Rate

Multiply the net taxable value by the locality's real estate tax rate per $100.

Example: $500,000 assessed value × $1.135 (Fairfax County rate) ÷ 100 = $5,675 annual tax

Virginia Property Tax Relief Programs

Virginia does not have a traditional statewide homestead exemption. Instead, the state mandates that localities offer certain relief programs and sets minimum standards — but each locality determines the actual benefit amounts, income limits, and application process.

Elderly and Disabled Tax Relief

Virginia law (§ 58.1-3210) requires every locality to offer a tax relief program for homeowners who are age 65 or older or totally and permanently disabled, subject to income and net worth limits set by the locality. The relief can take the form of:

  • A full exemption from real estate taxes
  • A partial exemption (percentage reduction)
  • A tax deferral (taxes deferred until the property is sold)
  • Any combination of the above

Benefits and income/net worth caps vary significantly by jurisdiction. Fairfax County, for example, offers relief on a sliding scale up to full exemption; rural counties may offer smaller reductions. Contact your locality's Commissioner of the Revenue to see what's available and apply before the deadline (usually March 1 or April 1).

Disabled Veteran Exemption

Veterans with a 100% service-connected disability rating — or rated individually unemployable — are exempt from all real estate taxes on their primary residence under Virginia Code § 58.1-3219.5. Surviving spouses of qualifying veterans may continue the exemption. No income limit applies. Apply at your local Commissioner of the Revenue with VA disability documentation.

Land Use Taxation

Virginia's Land Use Taxation Program (§ 58.1-3230) allows land actively used for agriculture, horticulture, forestry, or open space to be taxed based on its use value rather than fair market value. Use value for farmland is typically far below market value in growing areas, resulting in substantial savings for qualifying landowners. A rollback tax (up to 5 years of deferred taxes) applies if the land is converted to another use.

Surviving Spouse of First Responders Killed in the Line of Duty

Surviving spouses of police officers, firefighters, and other first responders killed in the line of duty qualify for a full real estate tax exemption on their primary residence for as long as they own it and do not remarry.

How to Appeal Your Virginia Property Tax Assessment

If you believe your property's assessed value is too high, Virginia gives you the right to appeal. Act within the filing windows — they are strict.

Appeal to the Commissioner of the Revenue

File a written appeal directly with your local Commissioner of the Revenue (or assessor, for localities that use a separate assessor). Present comparable sales data, a recent independent appraisal, or documentation of property defects. This informal review is the fastest path to a reduction.

Board of Equalization

Every Virginia locality has a Board of Equalization that meets annually to hear formal assessment appeals. File your appeal with the Board by the deadline specified on your assessment notice — typically within 3 months of the assessment date, or by a locally set deadline. The Board hears evidence and issues a written decision. Many appeals are resolved at this level.

Circuit Court

If you disagree with the Board of Equalization's decision, you may appeal to the local Circuit Court within 3 months of the Board's decision. This step involves formal legal proceedings and is used for high-value properties or significant valuation disputes.

When Are Virginia Property Taxes Due?

Virginia localities set their own tax due dates. Most jurisdictions use a two-installment schedule, but dates vary:

ScheduleCommon Due DatesLocalities
Two installmentsJune 5 and December 5Most counties and cities (default)
Two installmentsJune 15 and November 15Some cities (e.g., Richmond)
Single annual paymentDecember 5Some rural counties
QuarterlyJan, Apr, Jul, OctA few larger jurisdictions

Always verify the due dates with your specific locality — missing a payment date triggers interest and penalties that accrue quickly.

Payment Options

  • Online through your locality's treasurer or finance department website
  • In person at the county or city treasurer's office
  • By mail (postmark by the due date)
  • Drop box at the courthouse or government center

Bottom Line

Virginia's property tax system is more decentralized than any other state covered in this series — each of the 133 counties and independent cities sets its own rate, manages its own assessments, and designs its own relief programs. Your first priority after moving in is to verify your locality's elderly/disabled relief program if you qualify, and to check whether your land qualifies for use-value taxation if you own acreage. Use your parcel ID to pull up your jurisdiction's online records, confirm your assessed value, and verify any exemptions are applied. If your assessment seems high after a recent reassessment, the Board of Equalization is your most accessible and often most effective remedy — bring comparable sales and file before the deadline.
