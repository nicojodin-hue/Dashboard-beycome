Tennessee Property Tax Records: 25% Assessment Ratio, Tax Relief & County Assessor Lookup

Updated April 21, 2026 · 7 min read

Tennessee levies no state-level property tax on real estate — every dollar on your tax bill comes from your county and, if applicable, your city or special district. But what often surprises buyers is how Tennessee calculates the taxable amount: residential property is assessed at just 25% of appraised value, so the rate printed on your bill isn't applied to market value. Add in a state-funded Tax Relief program that can eliminate taxes for qualifying seniors, and Tennessee's property tax system rewards knowing the rules. This guide explains how to find your tax ID number, read your assessment, and reduce your bill in any of Tennessee's 95 counties.

What Is a Tennessee Property Tax ID Number?

Tennessee's property tax ID is called a Map-Group-Parcel number (also written as Tax Map Number, Parcel ID, or Account Number depending on the county). It is assigned by the county Assessor of Property — the elected official responsible for appraising all real and personal property in the county. The tax ID connects your property to its appraised value, ownership record, assessment class, and payment history in the county's records.

The same number may be labeled differently across counties:

  • Map-Group-Parcel (most common format statewide)
  • Parcel ID or Parcel Number
  • Tax Account Number
  • Property ID or PIN
  • GIS Parcel Number (in counties that use GIS-based systems)

How to Read a Tennessee Map-Group-Parcel Number

The standard Tennessee property tax ID follows a Map – Group – Parcel structure, derived from the county's tax map book system:

Map – Group – Parcel
Example: 012L A 003.00
Example (urban): 104 D 003.00
SegmentWhat It Represents
Map (012L)The assessor's tax map sheet covering a geographic area of the county
Group (A)A lettered subdivision of the map — a block, section, or grid cell
Parcel (003.00)The individual lot within that group; decimal suffix tracks splits and parent parcels

Large counties like Shelby (Memphis) and Davidson (Nashville Metro) use longer formats and may display the number differently in their portals. Always copy the exact format shown on your tax bill or assessor record — formats are not standardized across counties.

How to Find Your Tennessee Property Tax ID

All methods below are free.

1. Check Your Property Tax Bill

Tennessee counties mail annual tax bills in the fall (typically October). Your Map-Group-Parcel number is printed near the top, alongside your property address, appraised value, assessed value, and tax rate. This is the most direct source.

2. Search the Tennessee Property Assessment Data Portal (TPAD)

The Tennessee Comptroller of the Treasury operates the TPAD portal at assessment.cot.tn.gov/tpad — a centralized database covering 86 of the state's 95 counties. Select your county, then search by owner name, address, or parcel number. You'll see the appraised value, assessed value, tax class, and ownership details.

3. Use Your County Assessor's Portal

Nine of the most populous counties — Davidson, Hamilton, Knox, Shelby, Rutherford, Williamson, Montgomery, Chester, and Hickman — operate separate property search portals with additional data. Links are in the county directory at the end of this article.

4. Check Your Deed

Tennessee deeds are recorded with the County Register of Deeds (not a county recorder — the title is specific to Tennessee). Your deed references the Map-Group-Parcel number in the property description. Your title commitment and closing disclosure also include the parcel ID.

Tennessee's Assessment Ratio: The 25% Rule Explained

Tennessee assesses property at a fraction of appraised value based on use class. This is critical because the nominal tax rate on your bill is applied to the assessed value, not market value — so the advertised county rate significantly understates the real cost unless you account for the ratio.

Property ClassAssessment RatioWho It Affects
Residential (Class I)25% of appraised valueOwner-occupied homes, condos, most rentals
Farm (Class II)25% of appraised valueAgricultural land
Commercial / Industrial (Class III)40% of appraised valueBusinesses, warehouses, retail, investment property
Public Utility (Class IV)55% of appraised valueUtilities and infrastructure
Example: Home appraised at $400,000 × 25% = $100,000 assessed value. County rate of $2.30 per $100 × $100,000 ÷ 100 = $2,300 annual tax — an effective rate of 0.575% of market value.

The practical implication: when comparing Tennessee tax rates to states like Florida or Virginia (which assess at 100% of market value), you must multiply the Tennessee rate by the assessment ratio to make a fair comparison.

Tennessee Property Tax Relief Programs

Tennessee's Tax Relief program is unusually generous and structurally different from most states — it is funded directly by the state, not the county, meaning qualifying homeowners receive a reimbursement check or credit regardless of where in Tennessee they live.

Tax Relief for Elderly and Disabled

Tennessee reimburses property taxes on a primary residence for homeowners who are:

  • Age 65 or older, or
  • Totally and permanently disabled at any age

Income limits apply (currently $31,600 for most jurisdictions; Davidson County uses a higher local threshold). The state pays a fixed dollar amount per qualifying parcel — currently up to $134 per year for elderly and disabled homeowners, and up to $175 per year for disabled veterans. The reimbursement is applied directly to your tax bill; you pay only the remainder. Apply at your county trustee's office by April 5 of the tax year.

Tax Relief for Disabled Veterans

Veterans with a 100% permanent and total service-connected disability rating or who are listed as individually unemployable qualify for the veteran-specific tier of the Tax Relief program. The reimbursement is higher than the standard elderly/disabled amount and is funded entirely by the state. No income limit applies to the veteran exemption. Surviving spouses may also qualify. Apply at your county trustee's office.

Greenbelt Assessment (Agricultural, Forest, and Open Space)

Tennessee's Greenbelt Law (T.C.A. § 67-5-1001) allows agricultural land, forest land, and open space to be assessed based on current use value rather than market value. Use values are set annually by the State Board of Equalization based on productivity — often dramatically lower than market value for land near growing cities. If greenbelt land is converted to another use, a rollback tax applies for up to 5 years of deferred tax savings, plus interest.

How to Appeal Your Tennessee Assessment

Tennessee's appeal process is time-bound and follows a strict sequence. Miss the first deadline and you skip directly to a formal Board hearing.

Step 1: Informal Review with the County Assessor

Before the Board of Equalization session opens, contact your county Assessor of Property directly. Many assessors will correct obvious errors — wrong square footage, wrong property class, incorrect condition rating — informally. Bring your own comparable sales data or a recent appraisal.

Step 2: County Board of Equalization

The County Board of Equalization meets annually, typically June 1 through August 1 (specific dates vary by county). File your appeal with the County Board during this window. The Board hears evidence and issues a written decision. This is the most accessible level and resolves the majority of disputes.

Step 3: State Board of Equalization

If unsatisfied with the County Board's decision, you can appeal to the State Board of Equalization within 45 days of the County Board's order. The State Board conducts formal hearings through its Administrative Procedures Division and issues binding decisions. This level is used for high-value commercial properties and complex classification disputes.

When Are Tennessee Property Taxes Due?

Tennessee property taxes are assessed as of January 1. Counties mail bills in October. The payment schedule is uniform statewide:

EventDate
Tax bills mailedOctober 1 (approx.)
Taxes due (no penalty)Last day of February
Delinquent — 1.5%/month penalty beginsMarch 1
Tax sale process may beginAfter 1 year delinquent

Unlike most states, Tennessee does not split taxes into two installments — the full annual amount is due by the end of February. Some counties allow early payment discounts; check with your county trustee. Note that the county trustee (not the tax collector or treasurer) is the official responsible for collecting property taxes in Tennessee.

Payment Options

  • Online through the county trustee's portal (most counties accept credit/debit and e-check)
  • By mail to the county trustee's office (postmark by February 28)
  • In person at the county trustee's office
  • Drop box at the courthouse

Bottom Line

Tennessee's property tax structure quietly favors homeowners in two ways: the 25% residential assessment ratio keeps your taxable base at a fraction of market value, and the state-funded Tax Relief program can eliminate a meaningful portion of your bill if you're a qualifying senior, disabled, or veteran homeowner. Your county trustee's office handles both tax collection and Tax Relief applications — that's your first stop for any questions. If your assessed value looks off after a reappraisal year, the County Board of Equalization window (June – August) is the fastest and least formal path to a correction. Look up your Map-Group-Parcel number on the TPAD portal or your county's own system to verify your appraised value, confirm your classification, and make sure any applicable exemptions are active on your account.
